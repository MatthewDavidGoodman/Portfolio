The bounded tanh term prevents explosive prices; contract timing determines when physical tightness reaches reported revenue and EPS.

# AI/SEMI BOM-X
## Physical Demand, Earnings & Narrative Research Engine

**Prepared:** September 23, 2026  
**Core chain:** AI software demand -> compute -> semiconductor BOM -> capacity -> supplier earnings -> expectations -> trade.

## 1. Product definition
BOM-X is a quantamental research engine for AI infrastructure and semiconductors. It converts software usage, AI contracts, hyperscaler capex, accelerator roadmaps and infrastructure announcements into physical component demand, supplier revenue/EPS distributions, market-implied expectation gaps and event-driven research candidates.

It contains two linked but separate engines:

- **Structural physical-demand engine:** software usage/contracts/capex -> tokens/workloads -> accelerator-hours -> accelerators/racks -> HBM/DRAM/NAND/wafers/packaging/networking/power/cooling/WFE/test -> supplier revenue/EPS -> consensus/reverse-DCF gap.
- **Narrative/regime engine:** material AI/security/regulation events + rates/oil/credit + breadth/price action -> negative exhaustion, positive exhaustion, fundamental repricing, or no signal.

The central research variable is:

`StructuralAlpha(i,t) = EconomicImplication(i,t) - MarketExpectation(i,t)`

where `i` is a security/supplier and `t` is decision time.

## 2. Research thesis
Semiconductor and AI-infrastructure economics are constrained by engineering. A software or capex announcement must become accelerators, memory, storage, packaging, networking, electricity and buildings. The mapping is uncertain, but not arbitrary.

### 2.1 Component intensity
`ComponentDemand(c,t) = sum_p Units(p,t) * Content(c,p,t)`

- `c` = component class such as HBM, DDR, NAND, optics or power ICs.
- `p` = platform/system such as accelerator, server or rack.
- `Content(c,p,t)` = amount of component `c` required per platform `p` at time `t`.

### 2.2 Efficiency paradox
`TotalCompute(t) = Tasks(t) * ComputePerTask(t)`

Efficiency per task can improve while total compute demand rises if task volume, context, reasoning depth, multimodal work and agent loops grow faster.

## 3. Structural system architecture
1. **Software demand:** contracts, API spend, users, queries, tokens, context, model mix, agent loops.
2. **Compute:** model architecture, throughput/goodput, latency SLA, batching, utilization, spare capacity.
3. **BOM:** accelerators, HBM, host DRAM, NAND/SSD, wafers, advanced packaging, networking/optics, power/cooling.
4. **Supply:** fab capacity, yields, inventories, lead times, qualification, supplier share, substitution.
5. **Financials:** component units/ASP/share/fill rate -> revenue/margin/EPS.
6. **Expectations:** point-in-time consensus, analyst revisions, reverse DCF, price-implied assumptions.
7. **Narrative/regime:** material news, rates, oil, breadth, VWAP, positioning.

## 4. Contract/commitment and workload backsolvers
Use two independent routes and compare them.

### 4.1 Dollar route
`AccelHours = ComputeSpend / EffectiveCostPerAccelHour`

`InstalledAccel = PeakRequiredAccel / TargetUtilization`

Critical adjustments: software/cloud margin, utilization, goodput, existing spare capacity, peak/SLA reserve and hardware generation mix.

### 4.2 Workload route
`TokenDemand = Users * QueriesPerUser * TokensPerQuery * AgentAmplification`

Translate workload to compute using explicit model/hardware assumptions. Do not assume a universal FLOPs/token formula across dense, MoE, quantized and speculative-decoding systems.

`EffectiveThroughput = PeakThroughput * Efficiency * Goodput`

## 5. BOM translation
At minimum model:

- accelerator units by generation;
- HBM GB per accelerator;
- host DRAM GB/TB;
- NAND/SSD TB for raw/processed corpus, checkpoints, replicas, embeddings/vector stores, synthetic data, logs, persistent KV cache and scratch;
- foundry wafers from die area/node/yield;
- advanced packaging/interposer units;
- NICs, switches and optical transceivers;
- rack kW and site MW.

Core equations:

`HBM_GB = Accelerators * HBM_GB_PerAccelerator`

`SupplierRevenue(j) = ComponentUnits * ASP(j) * SupplierShare(j) * FillRate(j)`

## 6. Supply, scarcity and pricing
`Tightness(c,t) = (Demand(c,t) - EffectiveSupply(c,t)) / max(EffectiveSupply(c,t), epsilon)`

Effective supply adjusts nominal capacity for yield, downtime, qualification and mix. For memory, keep physical bit/unit demand separate from ASP/cycle effects.

`ASP(c,t) = PriceModel(Scarcity, InventoryDays, ContractReset, Mix, CycleState)`

## 7. Supplier earnings and expectations
`EPSGap(i,t) = E_BOMX[EPS(i,t+h)] - ConsensusEPS(i,t,h)`

Reverse DCF should solve for physical assumptions required by price, such as accelerator units, memory content, ASP, market share, gross margin and capex duration.

## 8. Rates/bonds and capex-duration overlay
Track nominal 10Y, real yields, 2Y, curve slope, IG spreads and relevant AI-related issuance. Separate:

1. direct project/debt sensitivity;
2. hyperscaler hurdle-rate sensitivity;
3. semiconductor second-order customer-capex sensitivity;
4. software valuation-duration sensitivity.

Oil is an event input when it changes inflation/rate expectations.

## 9. Narrative/event engine
The narrative engine is conditional and independently disableable.

### 9.1 AI-day filter
Require at least 2 of 3:
- shared AI/semiconductor catalyst;
- unusual AI-basket participation;
- unusual sector-relative movement.

### 9.2 Four states
- **Negative exhaustion:** bad narrative repeated, no new fundamental deterioration, tape/macro stabilizes.
- **Positive exhaustion:** good narrative crowded, participation deteriorates, early rally fades.
- **Fundamental repricing:** new information materially changes earnings/capex/technology/regulation.
- **No signal:** mixed/low-materiality information.

### 9.3 Monday hypothesis
Research workflow, not a hard rule:
- pre-open: overnight range, 10Y, crude, major AI headlines;
- 09:30-09:50 ET: form opening range; do not knife-catch;
- 09:50-10:45 ET: look for macro stabilization + VWAP reclaim + higher low + improving breadth;
- invalidate on morning-low break with fresh yield/oil highs or new fundamental deterioration.

### 9.4 Friday hypothesis
Test separately whether strong early Friday AI moves fade when incremental information is absent, breadth narrows, positioning is crowded or investors de-risk into the weekend. The model must be able to reject this hypothesis.

### 9.5 Regulation/security materiality
Treat an event as material only when it plausibly changes compute/chip access, model release/deployment cadence, enterprise adoption economics, liability/compliance, exportability, compute supply, or meaningful security/reliability assumptions.

## 10. Horizon separation
Use separate targets/models:

- `f1(X) -> residual return 1-5d`
- `f2(X) -> residual return 20d`
- `f3(X) -> residual return 60d`
- `f4(X) -> next-quarter EPS revision`

The narrative module must not rewrite a quarterly physical-demand forecast unless an event passes a structural materiality threshold.

## 11. State models
Structural state:

`z(t) = [D(t), K(t), B(t), E(t), M(t)]`

- D = physical demand
- K = effective productive capacity
- B = bottleneck/scarcity
- E = supplier earnings distribution implied by the physical system
- M = market-implied expectations

Event state:

`q(t) = [R(t), O(t), N(t), P(t), L(t)]`

- R = rates/credit financing regime
- O = oil/inflation impulse
- N = AI narrative/material-news state
- P = participation/breadth/leadership
- L = liquidity/positioning/exhaustion

## 12. Canonical data records
Time-version and provenance-tag: Event, Entity, Component, Platform, BOMEdge, CapacityState, FinancialSnapshot, ConsensusSnapshot, MarketState and ResearchOutput.

Every derived output must retain `as_of_timestamp`, source IDs, model version, assumption set, confidence/uncertainty and calculation trace.

## 13. Software architecture
Core packages: `ingest`, `normalize`, `graph`, `workloads`, `compute`, `bom`, `supply`, `financials`, `expectations`, `macro`, `narrative`, `signals`, `backtest`, `portfolio`, `api`, `dashboard`, `research`, `tests`.

Recommended MVP persistence: DuckDB + Parquet. Add PostgreSQL only when multi-user/streaming needs justify it.

## 14. First production experiment
**AI commitment -> component demand -> supplier EPS -> underreaction**

1. Start with NVDA/AMD/custom ASIC platforms; MU and SNDK for memory/storage; AVGO/networking; selected foundry/packaging exposures where public data are adequate.
2. Collect timestamped AI commitment, capex and usage events.
3. Run dollars-to-compute and workload-to-compute backsolvers.
4. Translate compute into accelerator/HBM/DRAM/NAND/networking/power distributions.
5. Run supplier share, fill-rate, ASP and margin scenarios into EPS.
6. Snapshot point-in-time consensus and price response.
7. Test residual EPS gap against 5d/20d/60d abnormal returns and next-quarter revisions.
8. Add learned BOM coefficients, state-space filters and GNNs only after the baseline works.

### Synthetic $100M test case
Illustrative medians only:
- $100M annual commitment
- 50% compute-cost share -> $50M compute cost
- $4/effective accelerator-hour -> 12.5M accelerator-hours
- 8,760 hours -> about 1,427 continuously utilized accelerator equivalents
- 60% target utilization -> about 2,378 installed accelerators
- 192 GB HBM/accelerator -> about 456 TB installed HBM

The production engine must use distributions, cross-check the token/workload route, and subtract usable spare capacity.

## 1. Non-negotiable engineering requirements

1. Use **Python 3.12+**.
2. The repository must run locally from a clean checkout.
3. Use `uv` or Poetry for dependency management; prefer `uv` unless there is a strong reason not to.
4. Use type hints everywhere meaningful and `pydantic` models for external/internal record validation.

6. Use `pytest`, `ruff`, `mypy` (or pyright), and pre-commit configuration.
7. All time series and events must be timezone-aware. Normalize storage timestamps to UTC and preserve original source timestamps/time zones.

   - `source_ids`

   - `confidence` or uncertainty representation
   - `calculation_trace_id`
9. Never silently impute a critical BOM, price, utilization, supplier-share or capacity input. Missing critical data must either:

11. No look-ahead leakage. Historical records and graph edges must be time-valid with `valid_from` and `valid_to` fields.
12. Favor interpretable baselines. Do not add RL, GNNs, LLM agents, or complex deep learning until the baseline engine and benchmark tests exist.
13. Define every mathematical variable in docstrings/docs when first used.
14. Keep model logic out of dashboards and notebooks. Core logic belongs in importable, tested modules.
15. Do not invent market data. Synthetic demo data must be clearly marked `synthetic=true`.

## 4. Structural engine: implement baseline first

### 4.1 Contract-dollar backsolver
Implement:

`accelerator_hours = compute_spend / effective_cost_per_accelerator_hour`

where:
- `compute_spend` is the share of commercial contract value economically attributable to compute;
- `effective_cost_per_accelerator_hour` is all-in cost per useful accelerator-hour.

Then estimate installed accelerators using:

`installed_accelerators = peak_required_accelerators / target_utilization`

Do NOT assume 100% utilization.

Inputs must support distributions (start with triangular, normal/lognormal where appropriate, and discrete scenarios). Return Monte Carlo quantiles and a trace of assumptions.

### 4.2 Workload/token backsolver
Implement a parallel route:

`token_demand = users * queries_per_user * tokens_per_query * agent_amplification`

Break input/output/reasoning tokens into separate fields where possible.

Create an extensible interface:

```python
class WorkloadModel(Protocol):
    def estimate_compute(self, workload: WorkloadSpec, hardware: HardwareSpec) -> ComputeDistribution: ...
```

The baseline may use explicit effective FLOPs/token or empirical tokens/sec parameters. Document limitations. Do not pretend one FLOPs/token formula is universal across dense/MoE, quantized or speculative-decoding systems.

### 4.3 Throughput/goodput
Represent:

`effective_throughput = peak_throughput * efficiency * goodput`

with explicit definitions:
- peak_throughput: theoretical hardware throughput;
- efficiency: achieved fraction after software/kernel/model limitations;
- goodput: useful completed work after failures/retries/latency overhead.

### 4.4 Spare-capacity adjustment
Incremental demand is not automatically incremental hardware.

Implement:

`incremental_hardware = max(0, required_capacity - existing_spare_capacity)`

but allow reliability/SLA reserve requirements that prevent full use of nominal spare capacity.

## 5. BOM engine

Create a time-versioned hardware/BOM registry.

At minimum support:
- accelerator units
- HBM GB
- host DRAM GB
- NAND/SSD TB
- advanced packaging units
- foundry wafer starts
- networking NICs/switch ports/optical transceivers
- power MW / rack kW

Core equations:

`component_demand(c,t) = sum_p units(p,t) * content(c,p,t)`

`HBM_GB = accelerators * HBM_GB_per_accelerator`

For wafers, model die area, wafer usable area, gross dies/wafer approximation and yield. Clearly label simplifications.

For NAND, do NOT reduce demand to raw training-dataset size. Include configurable drivers for:
- raw/processed corpus
- checkpoints
- experiment outputs
- replicas
- embeddings/vector stores
- synthetic data
- logs
- persistent KV cache
- scratch/local SSD

For memory/storage hierarchy, model HBM, DRAM and NAND separately. Allow workload-specific offload assumptions.

## 6. Supply/scarcity/ASP engine

Implement:

`scarcity = demand / effective_supply`

where effective supply adjusts nominal capacity for yield, downtime, qualification and product mix.

Baseline price model should be interpretable. Start with a monotonic piecewise or regression-style function using:
- scarcity
- inventory days
- contract reset cadence
- product mix
- cycle state

Do NOT hard-code a magical nonlinear function. Put coefficients in configuration, document them, and provide sensitivity plots/data.

Return both:
- physical unit/bit demand; and
- price/ASP effect.

This separation is mandatory for memory-cycle analysis.

## 10. Narrative/event engine

This is a separate package. It must be possible to disable it completely without affecting structural outputs.

### 10.1 Materiality classifier
Create deterministic baseline rules before ML.

A regulation/security/news event is material if it plausibly changes at least one of:
- legal access to compute/chips/equipment;
- model deployment/release cadence;
- enterprise adoption economics;
- liability/compliance cost;
- exportability/geographic availability;
- compute supply;
- significant security/reliability assumptions.

Low-impact tweets/social chatter should default to low materiality unless corroborated by a primary/reliable source or unusual multi-asset price action.

### 10.2 AI-day filter
Flag an AI-dominated session only when at least 2 of 3 are true:
1. shared AI/semiconductor catalyst;
2. unusual basket participation;
3. unusual sector-relative movement.

### 10.3 Four regimes
Implement enum/state probabilities for:
- NEGATIVE_EXHAUSTION
- POSITIVE_EXHAUSTION
- FUNDAMENTAL_REPRICING
- NO_SIGNAL

### 10.4 Monday hypothesis
Encode the research workflow, not a hard trading rule:
- pre-open overnight range, 10Y, crude, QQQ/SMH/NVDA;
- 09:30-09:50 ET opening range: do not knife-catch;
- 09:50-10:45 ET confirmation: macro stabilization + VWAP reclaim + higher low + improving breadth;
- invalidation: morning-low break with fresh yield/oil highs or new fundamental deterioration.

The module must produce an event-study score and test results. It must NOT output “buy because Monday.”

### 10.5 Friday hypothesis
Test separately whether strong AI-led early Friday moves fade when:
- no incremental information arrives;
- breadth narrows;
- positioning is crowded;
- investors de-risk into weekend.

Do not assume this effect exists. The backtest should be capable of rejecting it.

## 20. Coding style / research behavior

- Prefer small pure functions for financial/physical transformations.
- Use dataclasses/Pydantic models rather than unstructured dicts.
- Preserve units explicitly. Consider `pint` if it improves safety without excessive complexity.
- Log assumptions at INFO and model internals at DEBUG; never log secrets.
- Avoid global mutable state.
- Seed Monte Carlo RNGs through configuration for reproducibility.
- Use vectorized NumPy where appropriate, but keep formulas readable.
- Every model should have a `describe()` or trace output explaining its assumptions.
- Add docstrings with the economic interpretation, not just parameter types.
- If a requested advanced feature is not justified yet, create a clean interface and document why it is deferred instead of faking sophistication.
